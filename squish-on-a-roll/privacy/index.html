Squish on a Roll Privacy Policy

Effective date: 18 October 2026

Squish on a Roll is a tilt-controlled game for iPhone and Android made by Mister Dinguz and published by Keerae (see "Who we are"). This policy explains what the game does with information about you.

In short: the developer receives none of your game data. The game has no accounts and no analytics or crash reporting of its own, and your progress stays on your device. The game is free, and it shows optional ads from Google AdMob, only when you choose to watch one on the Game Over screen to get extra lives. To do that it includes Google's ad software, which collects the information described under "Ads" and shares it with Google to show and measure ads and to prevent fraud. The developer never sells anyone's information for money.

What stays on your device

Squish on a Roll saves a few things so the game can continue where you left off. These are stored only in the app's own private storage on your device:

  • Game progress: which stages you have completed, your ratings, the collectibles you have found and your best results.
  • Lives: how many lives you have and, while you are below the maximum, one reading of your device's clock: when the current wait for your next life began. That is normally the moment you lost a life with all your lives still left, or the time the game was opened if the device's clock had been turned back. The game uses it only to work out when your next life is due. It is a single value that is moved on as lives come back and deleted once your lives are full again; the game keeps no history of when or how long you play.
  • Settings: volume levels, control options such as tilt sensitivity and the on-screen jump button, vibration on or off.
  • Tilt calibration: the "neutral" angle you hold your phone at.
  • Damaged save files: if the game ever finds its save file damaged, it keeps a copy of the damaged file, named with the time it was found (up to three of them), next to your save, so that nothing is thrown away. These copies never leave the device either.

None of this is sent anywhere. Uninstalling Squish on a Roll deletes it. On Android the game opts out of Android's cloud backup. On iPhone, your saved progress may be included in the backups your device makes to iCloud or to your computer. Those backups are controlled by you and Apple, and Squish on a Roll cannot read them.

Motion sensors

You steer by tilting your phone, so while you play, Squish on a Roll reads your device's motion sensors (accelerometer, gravity and gyroscope). The readings are used on the device to move the ball and are then discarded. They are not recorded or sent anywhere. The only thing saved is the neutral holding angle described above.

Ads

When ads appear. An ad only plays when you tap Watch Ad on the Game Over screen, in return for 3 extra lives. Ads never interrupt play, and there are no banner, pop-up or other ads anywhere else in the game. You never have to watch one: lost lives also come back on their own, one every 10 minutes.

Who provides them. The ads come from Google AdMob, a service of Google LLC, through Google's Mobile Ads SDK and its User Messaging Platform, which Squish on a Roll includes through the open-source Poing Studios Godot AdMob plugin.

When the game contacts Google. Each time the game starts, it asks Google's consent service whether your consent is needed where you are, starts Google's ad software and fetches one ad so that it is ready. After each ad you watch it fetches the next one. While the game is open it also replaces the ready ad when that ad is about an hour old, because Google's ads expire. If a request fails or no ad is available (for example while you are offline), it tries again later while the game is open: after 30 seconds at first, then less often, down to once every 10 minutes, and also when the Game Over screen appears. It also contacts Google when you open Settings → Ad Privacy Options. Ads are only ever shown when you choose one.

Non-personalised ads only. Every ad request asks Google for non-personalised ads: ads chosen from the app and the general context, such as the approximate location of your internet connection, not from a profile of your interests. Google still uses device identifiers and cookies or local storage for non-personalised ads, to limit how often you see the same ad, to count ads in aggregate, and to detect fraud and abuse. Squish on a Roll also turns off Google's "publisher first-party ID", an identifier Google could otherwise keep for this app's ads.

No tracking on iPhone. Squish on a Roll never shows Apple's "Allow app to track" prompt, so it never permits tracking, and Google's software does not receive your iPhone's advertising identifier.

What Google receives. When the game uses Google's ad service, Google's software sends Google:

  • your device's IP address, which can show roughly where it is;
  • information about your device and the app, such as its model, operating system and version, language, and the app's version;
  • how each ad went (whether it loaded, was shown, finished, was closed or was tapped), and diagnostic and performance information about the ad software;
  • identifiers: on Android, your device's advertising ID (unless you have deleted it in your phone's settings) and an identifier Android gives this app; on iPhone, an identifier Apple gives the app's developer.

Who it goes to. The game shares information with no company but Google. To fill an ad request, Google may pass the request, without a profile of you, to other advertising companies that buy ad space through Google's systems, as Google's policies describe. Google protects that information at least as well as this policy describes. Google uses it to provide, measure and protect its ads, as described in Google's privacy policy, policies.google.com/privacy, and in "How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services", policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. How long Google keeps it is described at policies.google.com/technologies/retention.

If you tap an ad, it may open the advertiser's website or an app store page, outside the game. What happens there is covered by that site's or store's own policy.

Your choices.

  • In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form asks for your choice the first time the game starts there. Wherever your choices can be changed later (there, and in US states whose privacy laws give you the right to opt out), Settings shows an Ad Privacy Options button that opens them again.
  • If you do not consent, Google may still show limited ads, which use local storage on your device only to detect invalid traffic and fraud.
  • On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (for example Settings → Google → Ads, or Settings → Privacy → Ads).
  • You can always choose not to watch ads; the whole game works without them.

Report an ad. If you see an ad that seems inappropriate, or wrong for a younger player, email help@keerae.com with what it showed and roughly when. The Game Over screen shows this address too.

Permissions

  • Android: Squish on a Roll asks for vibration, for haptic feedback. Google's ad software adds the permissions it needs to fetch and show ads: internet access, network-state access, the advertising-ID permission (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID) and basic phone-state access (android.permission.READ_BASIC_PHONE_STATE, which reads only non-identifying facts such as the type of mobile network); and, through the background-work library Google's ad software uses, permission to keep the device awake briefly and to run background work (android.permission.WAKE_LOCK, android.permission.FOREGROUND_SERVICE). None of these shows a prompt.
  • iPhone: Squish on a Roll never shows a permission prompt, and never shows Apple's tracking prompt. Apple requires every app to describe camera, microphone and photo-library access if the app's code could use them. The game engine Squish on a Roll is built with contains that code, so the app includes those descriptions, and each one says that Squish on a Roll never uses that feature. Squish on a Roll never asks for any of them.

Third parties

Apart from Google's ad software described above, Squish on a Roll includes no third-party analytics, advertising, tracking or crash-reporting services. It is built with the open-source Godot Engine, which does not send any data from the game.

How long things are kept

  • What the game saves stays on your device until you uninstall the game.
  • If you email us, we keep your message and our reply for up to 12 months after your last message, then delete them. If we learn that a message came from a child under 13, we reply once (to a parent, if one wrote) and then delete it.
  • What Google keeps, and for how long, is described in Google's policies above.

Deleting your data

Uninstalling Squish on a Roll deletes everything it saved on your device. To have your emails to us deleted sooner, ask at help@keerae.com. For the information Google holds, use Google's own controls described in its privacy policy, and the choices above.

Your rights in the EU and UK

If you are in the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom or Switzerland, Keerae is the controller for the emails you send us, and Google is responsible for the information its ad software collects. The legal basis for the ad features is your consent, asked for by Google's consent form, for storing and reading information on your device and for the ads that use it; and the legitimate interest in funding a free game with optional ads, and in preventing fraud, where Google serves limited ads without consent. The legal basis for answering your emails is our legitimate interest in replying to you. You have the right to access, correct or delete your information, to object to or restrict its use, to withdraw consent at any time (Settings → Ad Privacy Options), and to complain to the data protection supervisory authority where you live or work (in the UK, the Information Commissioner's Office). Google LLC is based in the United States, and the information its software collects may be processed there; Google explains how it protects such transfers in its privacy policy.

Your rights in US states

Squish on a Roll never sells anyone's information for money. Where a US state's privacy law gives you the right to opt out of the sale or sharing of your personal information, as that law uses the words, Settings → Ad Privacy Options opens Google's form to do so. You can also ask us about your rights at help@keerae.com.

Children

Squish on a Roll is intended for players aged 13 and over. It does not knowingly collect personal information from children under 13, and the ads it requests are limited to ads rated no higher than PG. If you are a parent and have a question about a child's use of the game, write to help@keerae.com.
