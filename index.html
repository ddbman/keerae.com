Squish n Pops

400 levels · demo is done, page isn’t up yet

Hold your phone sideways. Tilt to steer, tap to fire. Every hit splits a bubble into two smaller ones, so a good shot basically hands you two new problems. Clear the screen and you’re out.

The demo is done. Ten levels, free, no ads.

The first seven teach you the basics. The last three skip ahead to where the weird bubbles start showing up. Beat all ten and you get a quick look at the late game. It runs right in your phone browser. No account, no payment, nothing to install. The itch.io page isn’t live yet. The second it is, the link goes right here.

This is SHORT FUSE, the last one in the demo. Set one bomb off and the chain pretty much plays the level for you.

HARD CANDY, where your first hit just rings off the shell. And the ten levels you get.

Nothing in this game ever speeds up. A bubble in level 200 moves at exactly the same speed as one in level 1, and each size always bounces back to the same height. All the difficulty is in how many there are, what they do, when they show up and how the room is shaped. Well, until 201. From there it’s the same 200 rooms again, zoomed way in, so you get a lot less warning. I wanted levels you could actually learn, not levels you barely survive.

iOS will only hand a web page the tilt sensors if you ask from inside a real tap, and only over HTTPS. So if someone says no, the game just flips to touch and lets them keep playing. I really didn’t want a permission popup to be the thing that ended somebody’s run.

Into Ember Depths

In development · desktop and browser

An online bullet-hell action RPG. You start out in a daylight town and fight your way inward through rings that keep getting darker, then head down into a descent that doesn’t end. Every floor’s boss opens the next one.

Every boss in the game, in no particular order. Seven up top, six in the dungeons, twenty down in the descent. Not a single palette swap.

The desktop build and the browser build run the exact same code now, and they play on the same server. They did drift apart for a while. I cut every cooldown so abilities come out of MP, tightened the aggro range, and gave the two easiest rings four map bosses each instead of one. Then I moved the browser build over to the new code, so I’m back to keeping one balance table in my head instead of two.

Rattlecup

Vertical slice, building for iOS and Android · working title

A liar’s dice roguelike. One rule does all the heavy lifting here. Are they telling the truth, or do I call them a liar? That question carries a whole run. Three floors of a crooked gambling house, your dice are your build, and every claim that misses gets paid out of your own health.

The art direction is called The Vigil. A candle, some faces, nothing else. Warm gold nearly everywhere, and red pretty much just means damage or the call. The opponents only got their own faces in September. Before that they were the same drawing three times over, which I could live with for a while and then really couldn’t.

Special dice and charms run on one effect system. Tricks and table modifiers are basically just data. So a new item is usually a line in a file, not new code. The smarter opponents read a public tell ledger and price you into it, which is still my favourite thing in there.

Rollaway

Running on real hardware · working title

A physics-based 3D tilt platformer for phones. Tilt to roll the ball, flick the phone upward to jump.

No screenshot yet, sorry. I’d rather show you nothing than show you a mockup. It did finally get off my desktop though, which was a good week. There are signed Android and iOS builds now, so the flick to jump I could only ever guess at in the editor is something I can hold in my hand and be wrong about.

Getting there meant building a verification chain that won’t ship a build it can’t vouch for. I found three ways it could have passed something it should have rejected, and closed all of them. Tedious work. Also the only reason I trust what’s on the phone.

Range

Yeah, it’s all me.

Four codebases and two engines. Into Ember Depths doesn’t even use an engine. Here’s what each tool is actually doing, and what I’d tell you about picking it if you asked.

ToolWhereWhat I’d tell you
TypeScript + Phaser 3 Squish n Pops The game logic never touches the artwork directly, so I can swap out the sprite art without changing any game code.
Electron Into Ember Depths It wraps the same code as the browser build, so one codebase ships to both. For an action game that costs you bundle size and memory. I knew that going in and took the trade anyway.
Canvas2D, no build step Into Ember Depths Plain ES modules, served exactly as they are. Nothing to compile means nothing sitting between making a change and seeing it.
Node + WebSockets Into Ember Depths One server, and any mix of desktop and browser players on the same wire.
Godot 4 + GDScript Rattlecup, Rollaway Picked it for the scene and component model. Rollaway’s levels get snapped together from a library of reusable parts instead of one-off scripts per level. Both games are on 4.7 now, and neither one moved until its whole test suite came back green on the new version.
Headless test suites All of them The game rules all get tested without a renderer. That’s the only reason I can make balance changes fast and still trust them.
